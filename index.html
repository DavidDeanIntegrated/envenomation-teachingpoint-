Arizona
Envenomation

Rattlesnake bites and bark scorpion stings: recognizing the moment supportive care becomes antivenom care.

Rattlesnake = tissue + blood + systemic Scorpion = neurotoxicity
Start with two patients
Case 1 • Rattlesnake
30-year-old, ankle bite
  • Pain + 4 cm swelling
  • Normal vital signs
  • Platelets and fibrinogen normal
  • No systemic symptoms
Case 2 • Scorpion
3-year-old, scorpion sting
  • Drooling
  • Roving eye movements
  • Intermittent jerking
  • Symptoms began within 20 minutes
Question for the room: Who needs antivenom right now?
Rattlesnake

Think: Tissue + Blood + Systemic

Local

  • Pain
  • Progressive edema
  • Ecchymosis / blistering

Hematologic

  • Thrombocytopenia
  • Low fibrinogen
  • Coagulation abnormalities

Systemic

  • Hypotension
  • Nausea / vomiting
  • Neurotoxicity in some venom phenotypes
A bite alone is not the indication. Progressive envenomation is.
Rattlesnake management

What am I doing in the first 15 minutes?

1

Mark + measure

Mark the leading edge of swelling and reassess serially.

2

Check the blood

CBC/platelets, PT/INR, fibrinogen ± additional labs guided by the presentation.

3

Call Poison Control early

Progressive local injury, clinically important hematologic toxicity, or systemic toxicity should trigger antivenom discussion.

Don’t: tourniquet • suction • cutting • electrical shock • ice

Arizona Poison Control advises observation even after an apparently dry bite because delayed envenomation can occur.

Arizona bark scorpion

Think: Neurotoxicity

Usually mild
Grade I–II

Local pain and paresthesias, sometimes extending away from the sting.

VERSUS
Severe
Grade III–IV

Cranial nerve dysfunction, skeletal neuromuscular dysfunction, or both.

Eyes

Roving or abnormal eye movements

Mouth

Drooling, dysarthria, dysphagia, tongue fasciculations

Muscles

Fasciculations, jerking, marked neuromuscular agitation

Scorpion management

The 3-year-old has the answer written all over the exam.

Drooling + roving eyes + jerking

That is severe neurotoxicity. Think airway, monitoring, toxicology, and Anascorp.

The bedside exam is the key diagnostic test.
Antivenom numbers worth remembering
CroFab
4–6

vials IV initially

Repeat until initial control. FDA labeling then recommends 2 vials q6h × 3 after control.

ANAVIP
10

vials IV initially

Repeat 10-vial doses if needed for initial control; 4-vial doses can be used for recurrent toxicity.

Anascorp
3

vials IV initially

If clinically important findings persist, give 1 additional vial every 30–60 minutes as needed.

Use local poison-center/toxicology guidance and the product labeling for the patient in front of you.

Optional board-style closer

Why does a 3-year-old often receive the same initial vial count as an 80-kg adult?

Because you are neutralizing the venom burden, not dosing a conventional drug by body weight.
